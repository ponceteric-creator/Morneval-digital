# Appendix J — Alternative Wealth Sources

**Status:** Locked design reference for the tabletop game. Numerical values explicitly stated below are locked for the current design and remain subject to later playtest rebalance. Simulation heuristics are identified separately.

**Relationship to other rules:** This appendix defines the current alternative routes to Family Wealth and supersedes older generic descriptions of Institution dividends where they conflict with the more specific rules below. Wealth remains a **capacity, not a banked currency**. Production-Sector Wealth remains governed by the economic-development rules; this appendix adds Temple Tithes, Scholarium Patents, and land-based Wealth discoveries.

---

## J.1 — Design Principle

Families must have several viable routes to Wealth. Production Stakes are an important source of Wealth, but they are **not intended to be mandatory** for every Family strategy.

The current Wealth ecology deliberately uses different mechanisms:

- **Production Stakes** create Wealth through productive/commercial activity.
- **Temple Tithes** transfer Wealth from prosperous Production Sectors to the holder of a religious levy.
- **Scholarium Patents** create a limited number of persistent intellectual-property rents while improving Morneval as a whole.
- **Military / City Guard land discoveries** attach permanent Wealth value to specific Hinterland territories and therefore make territorial ownership economically contestable.
- Existing contract mechanisms such as the **Mercenary Contract** remain separate sources of Wealth where defined by their own rules.

These sources are intentionally asymmetric. They should not all behave like Production Stakes.

# J.2 — Temple Tithes

## J.2.1 — Three Sector Tithes

There are three independent Tithe contracts, one for each refined Production Sector:

1. **Textile Tithe**
2. **Smithing Tithe**
3. **Construction Materials Tithe**

Each Tithe is auctioned separately in **Influence**, using the same general bidding structure as the Mercenary Contract.

A Family may win more than one Tithe in the same Generation, including all three. There is no one-Tithe-per-Family restriction.

## J.2.2 — Eligibility

A Family may bid for a Tithe only if it has **at least one Agent in the Temple** when the Tithe auction is resolved.

The number or seniority of Temple Agents does not by itself increase the size of the Tithe.

## J.2.3 — Activation Threshold

A Tithe applies only if its associated Production Sector generates at least **2 Wealth in total during that Generation**, measured before applying the Tithe.

If the Sector generates 0 or 1 Wealth, the Tithe has **no effect** for that Generation.

This threshold makes the Tithe self-regulating: it becomes valuable when a Sector is prosperous and naturally loses value when that Sector declines.

## J.2.4 — Tithe Effect

If the activation threshold is met, the Tithe holder chooses **one other Family** that generated at least 1 Wealth from Production Stakes in that Sector during the Generation.

Exactly **1 Wealth is transferred**:

- the chosen Family loses 1 Wealth from that Sector's generated Wealth;
- the Tithe holder gains +1 Wealth capacity.

The Tithe therefore **does not create new Wealth**. It redistributes Wealth generated by the Production Sector.

A Tithe can transfer at most **1 Wealth per Generation**, regardless of how much Wealth the Sector generated or how many Temple Agents the holder controls.

If no other Family is a valid payer, the Tithe has no effect.

## J.2.5 — Tabletop Targeting

In the tabletop game, the Tithe holder freely chooses which eligible Family pays the Tithe.

This makes the Tithe a targeted political/economic weapon rather than a purely passive income source.

The payer must still satisfy the normal eligibility condition: it must have generated at least 1 Wealth from the relevant Sector during that Generation.

## J.2.6 — Automated-Player Targeting

For simulation purposes, once a Tithe activates, the automated player targets the **eligible opposing Family with the highest Prestige**.

If the Tithe holder is itself the overall Prestige leader, it therefore normally targets the next-highest-Prestige eligible Family.

Only Families that actually generated Wealth from the relevant Sector are valid targets. If the nominal Prestige leader did not generate Wealth in that Sector, the AI moves to the highest-Prestige Family that did.

A final tie-break between equally prestigious eligible targets is a simulation parameter rather than a tabletop rule.

# J.3 — Scholarium Patents

## J.3.1 — Patent Principle

The Scholars' Collegium can generate Wealth through a small, finite set of **Patents** represented by Intrigue / Innovation cards.

A Patent has two components:

1. a **citywide technological effect** that benefits Morneval as a whole;
2. a **private ownership right** that provides +1 Wealth capacity to the Family that owns the Patent.

Patent effects apply to the entire city regardless of which Family owns them.

The Patent owner receives **+1 Wealth capacity per Generation per Patent owned**.

Because the Patent deck contains only a limited set of unique Patents, Patent-based Wealth generation is structurally capped by the game components rather than by an artificial numerical ceiling.

Patent ownership is represented visibly by a Family marker on the Patent card.

## J.3.2 — Patent Ownership and Intrigue

A Patent belongs to the owning **Family**, not to a particular Agent. Losing Scholarium representation does not automatically destroy the Patent.

Future Intrigue cards may allow Families to contest, steal, transfer or otherwise seize Patent ownership. The detailed resolution of those cards remains **TBD**, but when ownership changes the +1 Wealth income immediately follows the new owner.

The underlying citywide technological effect remains active unless a later card explicitly removes or suppresses it.

## J.3.3 — Definitive Patent List

### Advanced Farming Techniques

**Citywide effect:** each Civic Farm produces **+1 Raw Food**.

**Patent income:** the Patent owner receives **+1 Wealth capacity** each Generation.

This modifies Farm production only; ownership of Civic Farms remains public under the normal rules.

### Civic Sanitation Works

**Citywide effect:** after calculating Morneval's total Squalor normally, reduce final Squalor by **1**, minimum 0.

**Patent income:** the Patent owner receives **+1 Wealth capacity** each Generation.

The reduction is intentionally general rather than limited to Overcrowding. The card represents a broader package of sanitation, drainage, waste management and urban public works rather than only a sewer network.

### Advanced Judicial System

**Citywide effect:** Morneval receives **+1 Order**, subject to the normal Order maximum.

**Patent income:** the Patent owner receives **+1 Wealth capacity** each Generation.

### Advanced Architecture

**Citywide effect:** each Urban tile supports **4 Population instead of 3**.

Therefore:

> **Urban capacity = Urban tiles × 4**

while this Patent is active.

**Patent income:** the Patent owner receives **+1 Wealth capacity** each Generation.

Thematically, this represents improved engineering, multi-storey construction and denser urban architecture.

This Patent can have a particularly strong structural effect because it changes the capacity of every existing and future Urban tile.

# J.4 — Military / City Guard Land-Wealth Discoveries

## J.4.1 — General Rule

The Military / City Guard Intrigue deck contains unique discoveries that permanently improve a specific natural Hinterland territory.

When such a card is played, the Family chooses one eligible territory it currently controls. The improvement becomes **attached to that territory**, not to the Family.

An improved territory produces **+1 Wealth capacity per Generation for its current owner**.

This +1 Wealth is intrinsic to the land improvement and is **independent of whether the territory's raw resource is used by a Production Sector** during that Generation.

## J.4.2 — Ownership Transfer

If control of the improved territory changes, the attached Wealth improvement changes hands with it.

The new owner receives the +1 Wealth from that land under the normal timing for Wealth calculation.

This explicitly allows hostile territorial actions, including future **Hostile Land Takeover** Intrigue effects, to seize access to the Wealth source by seizing the land itself.

The exact resolution of Hostile Land Takeover cards is governed by the Intrigue-card rules and may be further detailed later.

## J.4.3 — Loss of the Improvement

The improvement exists only while the specific natural territory survives in a compatible form.

If that territory is:

- absorbed by Morneval and becomes **Urban**;
- converted into a public **Civic Farm**;
- destroyed or permanently removed by an Event or another rule;

then the attached improvement is lost and no longer generates Wealth.

The improvement does not remain with the previous owner and does not migrate to another territory.

## J.4.4 — Definitive Land-Wealth Cards

### Gemstone Vein

**Requirement:** choose one **Hill** you control.

A valuable gemstone deposit has been discovered on that territory.

**Effect:** that Hill now produces **+1 Wealth capacity per Generation** for its current owner.

### Rare Breed

**Requirement:** choose one **Meadow** you control.

Local stock breeders have developed an exceptional breed of livestock whose value extends beyond ordinary Wool production.

**Effect:** that Meadow now produces **+1 Wealth capacity per Generation** for its current owner.

### Precious Timber

**Requirement:** choose one **Forest** you control.

A rare tree species found only in this area produces timber of exceptional quality.

**Effect:** that Forest now produces **+1 Wealth capacity per Generation** for its current owner.

These three cards are unique improvements. Their physical attachment to terrain should remain visible so that players can identify valuable territorial targets.

# J.5 — Wealth-Source Summary

| Wealth route | Wealth created or transferred? | Persistence | Main dependency / risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Production Stakes** | Created by productive/commercial activity | Generational according to Stake/economy rules | Demand, production capacity, Stake ownership |
| **Temple Tithes** | **Transferred** from Production Wealth | One Generation per auction | Sector must generate at least 2 Wealth; requires Temple Agent; competitive Influence bid |
| **Scholarium Patents** | Created as Patent rent | Persistent while Patent is owned | Finite unique Patent set; ownership can later be contested/stolen |
| **Gemstone Vein / Rare Breed / Precious Timber** | Created by improved land | Persistent while territory survives | Land can change owner or be lost to Farm conversion / Urban expansion / destruction |
| **Mercenary Contract** | Contract income | According to contract rules | Eligibility, auction and operating requirements defined elsewhere |

# J.6 — Locked vs Open Points

## Locked

- Families must have viable Wealth routes beyond Production Stakes.
- There are three independent sector Tithes: Textiles, Smithing and Construction Materials.
- Tithe eligibility requires at least one Temple Agent.
- Tithes are awarded by Influence bidding and may all be held by the same Family.
- A Tithe activates only at **2+ total Wealth generated by its Sector**.
- An active Tithe transfers exactly **1 Wealth** from an eligible producer chosen by the holder.
- In tabletop play, the holder chooses the payer.
- In simulation, the AI targets the highest-Prestige eligible opposing Family.
- There are four citywide Scholarium Patents: **Advanced Farming Techniques, Civic Sanitation Works, Advanced Judicial System, Advanced Architecture**.
- Each Patent provides **+1 Wealth capacity** to its owner while its citywide effect applies to all Morneval.
- Advanced Farming Techniques gives **+1 Food per Civic Farm**.
- Civic Sanitation Works gives **−1 total Squalor**, minimum 0.
- Advanced Judicial System gives **+1 Order**, subject to the normal maximum.
- Advanced Architecture changes Urban capacity from **3 to 4 Population per Urban tile**.
- There are three unique land-Wealth discoveries: **Gemstone Vein, Rare Breed, Precious Timber**.
- Each attaches +1 Wealth to one eligible Hill, Meadow or Forest respectively.
- Land-Wealth improvements transfer with territory ownership and disappear if the territory is converted, urbanized or destroyed.
- Land-Wealth income does not depend on the territory's raw resource being consumed by a Production Sector.

## Still Open / Future Design

- Exact Intrigue-card acquisition timing and access conditions for the four Patents and three land-discovery cards, beyond their Institution-deck identity.
- Exact cards and procedures for contesting or stealing Patents.
- Exact Hostile Land Takeover card procedure.
- Final simulation bidding heuristics for valuing each Tithe before Production resolution.
- Final tie-break used by the automated player between equally prestigious valid Tithe targets.

These open points must not be inferred as fixed rules until separately designed and locked.
