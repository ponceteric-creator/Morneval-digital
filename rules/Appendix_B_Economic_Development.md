# Appendix B — Economic Development, Hinterland & Urban Growth (v0.8.9)

**Status:** current design reference for the v0.8 economic-development prototype.  
**Relationship to master rules:** this appendix supplements `Morneval_Rules.md`; where it provides newer detail on Production-Sector development, Food, Imperial demand, Hinterland exploration, Squalor or Renown, this appendix is the current specification until the next master-rule consolidation. **Urban expansion is now governed by [`Appendix_C_Civic_Expansion_Vote.md`](./Appendix_C_Civic_Expansion_Vote.md), which supersedes the former automatic-expansion procedure.**

## B.1 — Production-Sector development projects

Advancing a Production Sector from Tier I → II or Tier II → III requires a **three-phase development project**. Different Families may fund successive phases, but a given Sector project may advance by at most **one phase per Generation**.

| Phase | Influence cost | Wealth-capacity cost | Current Prestige reward |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 5 |
| 2 | 0 | 2 | 5 |
| 3 | 0 | 2 | 5 |

After Phase 3, the new Tier activates. The 5-Prestige reward remains provisional.

## B.2 — Wealth is capacity; Family base Wealth

Wealth is a **capacity, not a banked currency**. Every Family has permanent **base Wealth capacity 1** each Generation. Wealth generated by serving External demand is added to that capacity for the Generation.

Wealth committed to development, exploration, Civic Farm conversion or other Wealth-funded actions occupies that capacity for the Generation.

## B.3 — Production as refinement

Food is **not** a Production Sector. There is no Refined Food chain and no City, Imperial or External market demand for Food.

The active Production Sectors are:

- **Textiles** — uses Wool from Meadows;
- **Smithing** — uses Ore from Hills;
- **Construction Materials** — uses Wood from Forests.

Each occupied Production Stake represents one unit of potential refined production and can satisfy at most one need, subject to available raw input.

## B.4 — Demand categories and rewards

Textiles, Smithing and Construction Materials each have:

- **City demand**;
- **Imperial demand**;
- **External demand**.

Imperial demand is permanently **1 per Sector per Generation**.

Current direct rewards:

| Demand served | Reward to Stake owner |
|---|---:|
| City | +1 Prestige |
| Imperial | none |
| External | +1 Wealth capacity |

If any Imperial demand remains unmet anywhere at Generation end, every Family loses **1 Prestige**, minimum 0. This is a single Generation-wide penalty.

## B.5 — Demand priority and City Inclination

Only the Military ↔ Commercial axis currently affects economic allocation:

- **Military:** City → Imperial → External;
- **Commercial / Mercantile:** External → Imperial → City;
- **Neutral:** Imperial → City → External.

The Academic / Arcane ↔ Religion axis currently has no production-allocation effect.

## B.6 — Raw Food and Population subsistence

Food is a **pure raw subsistence resource**.

Each Civic Farm produces **2 Raw Food**. Each Population consumes **1 Raw Food**.

Therefore:

- 1 Farm supports 2 Population;
- 2 Farms support 4 Population;
- 3 Farms support 6 Population;
- and so on.

Raw Food is used only to feed Population. Agricultural surplus is not refined, does not satisfy market demand and does not create Wealth through a Production Sector.

Population grows by **+1** only if:

1. local Civic Farms fully feed the current Population without Imperial Food aid; and
2. freshly calculated **Squalor is strictly lower than current Population**.

If Squalor ≥ Population, Population does not grow that Generation.

## B.7 — Imperial Food aid

If local Civic Farms cannot feed the current Population, the **Empire automatically supplies all missing Raw Food**.

There is no famine Population loss from Food shortage and Morneval cannot fall below **Population 1**.

Whenever any Imperial Food aid is used:

- Population does not grow that Generation;
- every Family loses **1 Prestige**, minimum 0.

The size of the shortfall does not increase the Prestige penalty.

## B.8 — Hinterland map and hidden exploration

The map contains:

- 1 original Morneval city space;
- 12 surrounding Hinterland spaces.

All 12 Hinterland spaces begin unexplored. The hidden terrain pool contains exactly:

- **4 Forests**;
- **4 Meadows**;
- **4 Hills**.

Exploration reveals one random remaining terrain from this finite pool.

## B.9 — Explore a Hinterland territory

A Family may explore one unrevealed Hinterland space for:

- **3 Influence**;
- **1 Wealth capacity for that Generation**.

The exploring Family immediately controls the revealed territory. Exploration order is recorded.

Each newly revealed natural territory currently has **2 raw-resource capacity**.

Natural terrain produces:

- Meadow → Wool;
- Hill → Ore;
- Forest → Wood.

There is no free city-adjacent base raw production.

## B.10 — Civic Farm conversion — locked rule

A Family may convert one revealed natural territory it controls into a **Civic Farm**.

Current conversion cost:

- **2 Influence**;
- **1 Wealth capacity for that Generation**.

The contributing Family immediately gains **+3 Prestige**.

After conversion, ownership is surrendered. The Farm becomes **public property of Morneval** and is no longer controlled by any Family.

A Civic Farm:

- produces **2 Raw Food** for Population subsistence;
- serves the whole city, not a particular Family;
- has no Production-Sector Stake chain;
- has no City, Imperial or External market demand;
- generates **no recurring productive-land Prestige**;
- may later be absorbed by an approved Urban-expansion vote like any other Hinterland territory.

The original terrain remains recorded for future Events and External-Power interactions.

This rule deliberately creates a public-good trade-off: retaining Forest/Meadow/Hill preserves a private productive asset, whereas converting it to a Farm exchanges that private engine for an immediate Prestige reward and shared food security.

The +3 Prestige value is the current locked test value. +4 remains a possible future balance lever if Civic Farms prove systematically unattractive.

## B.11 — Productive Hinterland Prestige

A Family gains **1 Prestige** for each controlled natural Hinterland territory whose output is actually used during the Generation.

This applies to Wool, Ore and Wood used by Production Sectors.

**Civic Farms never generate recurring productive-land Prestige**, because they cease to be Family property immediately upon conversion.

## B.12 — Urban capacity and political expansion — v0.8.9

Morneval begins with **1 Urban tile**, supporting **3 Population**.

**Urban capacity = Urban tiles × 3.**

Urban expansion is **not automatic**. When Population reaches or exceeds current Urban capacity, a Family may propose expansion through the Civic Expansion Vote defined in Appendix C.

At most one Urban tile can be added in a Generation. If no proposal is made or the vote fails, Morneval remains at its current Urban capacity even if Population exceeds that capacity.

This means Overcrowding may persist and directly contribute to Squalor.

## B.13 — Territory absorbed by an approved expansion

If a Civic Expansion Vote passes, the city absorbs exactly **one** Hinterland territory: the **oldest explored non-urban territory**, according to recorded exploration order.

When that territory is absorbed:

- any Family ownership is lost;
- public Civic Farms can also be absorbed;
- no compensation is paid;
- all raw production is permanently lost;
- the territory becomes Urban permanently;
- Urban capacity increases by **+3 Population**.

For proposal eligibility, vote procedure and AI voting behaviour, see Appendix C.

## B.14 — Direct Squalor calculation — locked rule

Squalor has **no target, no gradual movement and no inherent persistence**. It is recalculated directly each Generation from current conditions.

After Production and demand have been resolved, but before Population growth and Disease:

**Overcrowding = max(0, current Population − current Urban capacity).**

Then total all unmet City demand across Textiles, Smithing and Construction Materials:

**Unmet City demand = sum of all City needs requested but not served.**

Set:

**Squalor = Overcrowding + Unmet City demand.**

Each unmet City need therefore creates +1 Squalor immediately. Food is not included because Raw Food is a subsistence requirement rather than a Production-Sector demand.

Imperial and External unmet demand do not directly add Squalor.

The resulting Squalor is used immediately for both Population growth and Disease.

Disease uses the current provisional table:

| Squalor | Disease chance |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0% |
| 1 | 0% |
| 2 | 5% |
| 3 | 10% |
| 4 | 20% |
| 5 | 35% |
| 6+ | 50% |

Disease can reduce Population by 1, subject to the current minimum Population of 1.

## B.15 — Current demographic / expansion sequence

1. At the beginning of the Generation, if `Population ≥ Urban capacity`, resolve at most one Civic Expansion proposal/vote under Appendix C.
2. Resolve Raw Food availability and Imperial Food aid status using the post-vote map.
3. Resolve Textiles, Smithing and Construction Materials and their demand.
4. Calculate Squalor directly from Overcrowding + unmet City demand.
5. Check Population growth using that freshly calculated Squalor.
6. Resolve Disease using that same Squalor.
7. Determine final Population.
8. **Do not perform any automatic Urban expansion at Generation end.** A later expansion requires a future Civic Expansion Vote.

## B.16 — Renown cap

Until Events become the true source of Renown, the prototype keeps temporary automatic **+1 Renown every 2 Generations**.

**Maximum Renown = final Population × 2.**

A direct Squalor-to-Renown penalty remains parked.

## B.17 — Automated-player heuristic

The automated strategy is a testing heuristic, not a tabletop rule.

The AI can:

- explore a random Hinterland territory;
- convert controlled natural land into a Civic Farm when local food security requires it;
- bid for Production Stakes in Textiles, Smithing and Construction Materials;
- advance those Production Sectors;
- pass;
- propose, support, oppose or abstain on a Civic Expansion Vote.

Farm conversion is treated as a food-security action. Once converted, the Farm becomes public, the contributor receives the one-time +3 Prestige reward, and no recurring Farm Prestige exists.

For expansion voting, the AI compares civic benefits with the destruction of productive Hinterland. It values relief of Overcrowding and future population headroom, penalises loss of raw capacity, strongly penalises destruction of its own private productive territory, and accounts for Food-security consequences if the target is a Civic Farm. Higher estimated stakes can cause the AI to spend more Influence on YES or NO.

The legacy automatic expansion routine remains present below the current wrapper for compatibility but its results are explicitly reversed; in v0.8.9 the Civic Expansion Vote is the **only** route to a new Urban tile.

Imperial demand is valued by the AI as avoidance of the collective Prestige penalty; this remains a simulation heuristic only.
