# Appendix B — Economic Development, Hinterland & Urban Growth (v0.8.4)

**Status:** current design reference for the v0.8 economic-development prototype.  
**Relationship to master rules:** this appendix supplements `Morneval_Rules.md`; where it provides newer detail on Production-Sector development, Food, Imperial demand, Hinterland exploration, Squalor, Urban expansion or Renown, this appendix is the current specification until the next master-rule consolidation.

## B.1 — Production-Sector development projects

Advancing a Production Sector from Tier I → II or Tier II → III requires a **three-phase development project**.

Different Families may contribute successive phases. Contribution is first-come-first-served and a given Sector project may advance by at most **one phase per Generation**.

| Phase | Influence cost | Wealth-capacity cost | Current Prestige reward |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 5 |
| 2 | 0 | 2 | 5 |
| 3 | 0 | 2 | 5 |

After Phase 3, the new Tier activates. The **5 Prestige per phase remains provisional**.

## B.2 — Wealth is capacity; Family base Wealth

Wealth remains a **capacity, not a banked currency**.

As of v0.8.4, every Family has a permanent **base Wealth capacity of 1** each Generation, representing the independent resources of the dynasty. Wealth generated by serving External demand is added to this base capacity for that Generation.

Wealth committed to development, exploration, Farm conversion or other Wealth-funded actions occupies that capacity for the Generation.

## B.3 — Production as refinement

Production Sectors represent **refinement / finished production**, not extraction.

The current Sectors are:

- **Refined Food** — uses agricultural surplus after Population subsistence;
- **Textiles** — uses Wool from Meadows;
- **Smithing** — uses Ore from Hills;
- **Construction Materials** — uses Wood from Forests.

One Production Stake can satisfy at most one unit of demand, subject to the Sector's Stake slots and available raw input.

## B.4 — Demand categories and rewards

### Refined Food

Refined Food has:

- **City demand**;
- **External demand**;
- **no Imperial demand**.

City demand served awards **1 Prestige** to the serving Stake owner.

External demand served awards **1 Wealth capacity** to the serving Stake owner.

### Non-Food Sectors

Textiles, Smithing and Construction Materials each have:

- **City demand**;
- **Imperial demand**;
- **External demand**.

Imperial demand is permanently **1 per Sector per Generation**.

Meeting Imperial demand gives **no direct reward**. In the current digital implementation, if **any Imperial demand remains unmet anywhere at Generation end**, every Family loses **1 Prestige**, minimum 0. This is a single Generation-wide penalty, not one penalty per unmet Sector.

External demand awards **1 Wealth** per need served. City demand awards **1 Prestige** per need served.

## B.5 — Demand priority and City Inclination

Only the Military ↔ Commercial axis currently affects economic allocation.

- **Military** prioritises City demand.
- **Commercial / Mercantile** prioritises External demand.
- **Neutral** prioritises Imperial demand.
- The Academic / Arcane ↔ Religion axis currently has **no production-allocation effect**.

The current deterministic residual order is:

- Military: City → Imperial → External;
- Commercial: External → Imperial → City;
- Neutral: Imperial → City → External.

Refined Food has no Imperial demand, so the Imperial step is simply skipped.

## B.6 — Raw Food and Population subsistence

Raw Food is **not** produced by the Refined Food Sector.

Each Farm currently produces **2 Raw Food**, and each unit of Population consumes **1 Raw Food**.

Therefore:

- 1 Farm supports 2 Population;
- 2 Farms support 4 Population;
- 3 Farms support 6 Population;
- and so on.

Raw Food is always allocated to **Population subsistence first**. Only Farm capacity left after feeding the Population can be used as raw input by the **Refined Food** Production Sector.

If local Farms fully feed the current Population, Population may grow by **+1** under the current prototype growth rule.

## B.7 — Imperial Food aid

If local Farms cannot feed the current Population, the **Empire automatically supplies all missing Raw Food**.

There is therefore no famine loss from Food shortage and Morneval cannot fall below **Population 1**.

If any Imperial Food aid is required during a Generation:

- Population does **not** grow from Food that Generation;
- every Family loses **1 Prestige**, minimum 0.

The amount of missing Food does not increase this Prestige penalty.

## B.8 — Hinterland map and hidden exploration

The map abstraction contains:

- 1 original Morneval city space;
- 12 surrounding Hinterland spaces.

At setup, all 12 Hinterland spaces are **unexplored**. Their terrain is unknown.

The hidden terrain pool contains exactly:

- **4 Forests**;
- **4 Meadows**;
- **4 Hills**.

Exploration reveals one random remaining terrain from this finite pool.

## B.9 — Explore a Hinterland territory

A Family may explore one unrevealed Hinterland space for:

- **3 Influence**;
- **1 Wealth capacity for that Generation**.

The terrain is then revealed randomly and the exploring Family immediately controls the territory.

The game records the **exploration order** of all revealed territories.

Each newly revealed natural territory currently has **2 raw-resource capacity**. A future development mechanism may improve an individual territory to capacity 3.

Natural terrain produces:

- Meadow → Wool;
- Hill → Ore;
- Forest → Wood.

There is **no free city-adjacent base raw production** in v0.8.4.

## B.10 — Converting land to a Farm

A Family may convert a revealed natural territory it controls into a Farm for:

- **2 Influence**;
- **1 Wealth capacity for that Generation**.

The Farm retains the territory's current capacity value, currently 2, but stops producing its original resource and instead produces Raw Food.

The original terrain remains recorded for future Events / External Power interactions.

## B.11 — Productive Hinterland Prestige

The existing productive-land principle remains active: a controlled Hinterland territory whose output is actually used awards its Family **1 Prestige** for that Generation.

In v0.8.4, the digital implementation counts Farm output used directly for Population subsistence as productive use as well as raw input consumed by a Production Sector. This is a **prototype interpretation** of the productive-land rule and should be revisited if it produces excessive Farm Prestige.

## B.12 — Urban capacity and expansion

Morneval begins with **1 Urban tile**, supporting **3 Population**.

**Urban capacity = Urban tiles × 3.**

After final Population is known, including disease:

**Required Urban tiles = ceil(final Population / 3), minimum 1.**

If more Urban tiles are required, Morneval permanently absorbs Hinterland.

Urbanisation is irreversible.

## B.13 — Which territory is absorbed

As of v0.8.4, expansion no longer chooses unowned land first.

The city absorbs the **oldest explored non-urban territory first**, according to recorded exploration order.

This creates an abstract spatial model in which the earliest explored territories are treated as those closest to the original settlement.

When a territory is absorbed:

- its Family loses control;
- there is **no compensation**;
- all of that territory's raw production capacity is permanently lost;
- it becomes Urban and never returns to Hinterland.

## B.14 — Overcrowding-driven Squalor

Squalor is driven by Population exceeding existing Urban capacity.

Before disease:

**Overcrowding = max(0, Population − current Urban capacity).**

Current target:

**Squalor target = Overcrowding.**

Squalor moves by at most **1 point per Generation** toward this target.

Because the Empire prevents Food shortage from becoming actual famine, Food shortage no longer adds a separate Squalor penalty in v0.8.4.

Disease is then checked using the existing provisional table:

| Squalor | Disease chance |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0% |
| 1 | 0% |
| 2 | 5% |
| 3 | 10% |
| 4 | 20% |
| 5 | 35% |
| 6+ | 50% |

Disease can reduce Population by 1, but Population has a hard minimum of 1 in the current prototype.

## B.15 — Renown cap

Until Events become the true source of Renown, the prototype keeps temporary automatic **+1 Renown every 2 Generations**.

Sustainable Renown is capped at:

**Maximum Renown = final Population × 2.**

A direct Squalor-to-Renown penalty remains parked.

## B.16 — Balance graphs

The v0.8.4 dashboard tracks:

- Population and Urban capacity;
- Squalor;
- Renown;
- Family Wealth, including the permanent base Wealth 1;
- cumulative Family Prestige after Imperial penalties;
- gross revealed raw production for Raw Food, Wool, Ore and Wood.

The raw-production graph records actual revealed productive capacity after urban expansion. Hidden unexplored terrain does not appear as production.

## B.17 — Automated-player heuristic

The automated strategy is a testing heuristic, not a tabletop rule.

The AI can:

- explore a random Hinterland territory;
- convert controlled natural land into a Farm;
- bid for Production Stakes;
- advance Production-Sector development;
- pass.

The AI sees strategic pressure but **cannot choose the terrain revealed by exploration**.

Imperial demand is valued by the AI as avoidance of its own 1-Prestige penalty; this is only a simulation heuristic for bidding decisions.
